Use as much (or as little) of the app as you want

Sometimes you want to see the whole answer, and sometimes you want to type something and have the app vanish. There are four layouts, and you can switch between them from the menu bar.

fig. 2

Calypso.

The incognito mode, and my favourite. It's a thin dark strip with no buttons or chat history. Type your question, press Return, and the strip disappears while a small mark in a corner of the screen tells you when the answer's ready.

Visibility goes from 5% to 100%, and you pick which corner the mark sits in. It's discreet, not invisible: screen sharing and recordings still show it.

fig. 3

Box.

The everyday one. Paste a huge block of text and it gets tucked into a small chip so the box stays readable. Only the answer gets copied.

fig. 4

Sidebar chat.

For when you need a follow-up. It sits along the side of your screen, keeps the conversation going, and every answer has its own Copy button.

fig. 5

Minimal.

Just the box you type into. Everything else waits behind the ⋯ button.

A few more things it can do

These aren't things you have to set up or learn on day one. Just useful little extras.

In the menu bar

Your last 10 answers.

Copied something else over the answer you just got? The last ten are in the menu bar, and one click copies it again. Answers you get in Calypso aren't kept here unless you turn that on.

You can tell when it's offline.

The bud icon turns grey when there's no connection.

Settings are here too.

Model, layout and Settings all live in the menu. Calyx doesn't put an icon in your Dock.

In the Editor

Offline mode.

With macOS 26 and Apple Intelligence switched on, Calyx can use the AI built into your Mac when you're offline. You don't pay anything for those answers, and the question stays on your Mac. Treat it as a backup rather than a Claude replacement.

See what you just copied.

A few words under the box describe the answer, so you know what you're about to paste. They come back with the answer itself, so they don't cost an extra request.

Want the full answer first?

Click that little summary to read it before pasting.

Drag files in.

PDFs, screenshots, Word documents, PowerPoints, spreadsheets and most code files all work.

Across your Mac

Screenshots with a shortcut.

⌃Space grabs what's on the screen. Press it again to add another screenshot. Calyx doesn't appear in its own captures.

It takes the whole screen your mouse is on. You can switch to dragging out an area in Settings › Shortcuts.

It can paste for you.

⌃⇧Space sends your question and types the answer into the text field you were using, so you skip the paste step too.

Esc twice resets your Wi-Fi.

One Esc closes the Editor. Two quick ones switch Wi-Fi off and put Calyx away, menu-bar icon and all. ⌥Space turns Wi-Fi back on and brings Calyx back. You can turn this off in Settings › Shortcuts.

It stays where you put it.

It floats over full-screen apps and shows up on every desktop. Each layout remembers where you dragged it, and it opens on whichever screen your mouse is on.

What stays on your Mac

  • Your API key.Stored in a folder only your user account can access. Calyx sends it only to the AI provider you've chosen.
  • Offline questions.If you're using Apple's on-device model, your question is processed on your Mac.
  • Your activity.You don't need a Calyx account, and the app doesn't have analytics.

What you need

  • An Apple silicon MacM1 or newer.
  • macOS 14 or laterFor everything except offline mode.
  • macOS 26 with Apple Intelligence onFor offline mode, using the AI built into your Mac.
  • An Anthropic or OpenAI API keyFor answers from Claude or OpenAI. The provider bills you for what you use.
  • Screen Recording and AccessibilityOnly if you want screenshots and pasting for you. Both are optional.
